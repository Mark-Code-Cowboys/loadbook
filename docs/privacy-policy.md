# Loadbook Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** (set at publication)
**Publisher:** Code Cowboys LLC
**Published at:** https://code-cowboys.com/privacy/loadbook

Loadbook is a personal reloading notebook. Our privacy policy is short
because the app is built so that we *cannot* see your data.

## The short version

Everything you record in Loadbook — cartridges, loads, components,
range sessions, inventory, notes, photos — is stored **only on your
device**. Loadbook has no accounts, no analytics, and no advertising.
We do not collect, transmit, store, sell, or share any personal data.
We could not hand your notebook to anyone even if asked, because we
never have it.

## Card scanning

The index-card import photographs your own cards and pages and reads
them **on your device** (Google ML Kit's bundled, offline text
recognition). Card images and everything read from them stay on your
phone. Nothing is uploaded — there is nowhere to upload it to.

## Camera and photos

Camera and photo-library access is used only when you scan cards or
attach a target photo to a range session. Images you capture are
stored locally with the rest of your notebook. The target measurer
runs entirely on your device.

## Location

Loadbook requests **no location permission** and never reads your
device's location.

## Sharing, export, and backup

CSV export and the backup file are created only when you tap the
button, and go only where you send them via your phone's share sheet.
Restoring a backup reads only the file you pick. We are not party to
any of it.

## Purchases

Loadbook Pro is sold through Google Play (and, on iOS, the App Store).
The store processes the payment and tells the app only "Pro is owned."
We receive no payment details. Store purchase traffic is the app's
only network use.

## Children

Loadbook is a record-keeping tool for adults engaged in a regulated
hobby and is not directed at children.

## Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new text will be published at the URL
above with a new effective date. Since the app collects nothing, we
expect it to stay boring.
